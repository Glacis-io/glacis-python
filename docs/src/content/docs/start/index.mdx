You do not need to install anything to see what a Glacis **record** is and check
one for yourself. Three steps, all in a browser, all of it fine on a phone.

<Aside type="note" title="Record vs receipt">
  Human-facing copy says **record**. The OVERT specification and some SDK field
  names still say *receipt* — same artifact.
</Aside>

<Steps>

1. **[Create a workspace](/start/create-a-workspace/)** — email and a password at
   [app.glacis.io](https://app.glacis.io). About a minute, including the
   verification email.

2. **[Look at the sample records](/start/sample-workspace/)** — your new workspace
   is not empty. It comes with synthetic records, every one badged
   <ReceiptBadge kind="sample" />, so you can see the shape before you produce one.

3. **[Mint a real one](/start/mint-a-receipt/)** — describe an event, get back a
   record bound to your workspace, and check it yourself.

</Steps>

Then, when you want records to come out of your own system instead of a form,
[Connect](/connect/) is the ten-minute version: a config block or three lines of
Python.

## What a record is

A record is **cryptographic evidence that the specific claims stated in it were
generated for a given event**, at a point in time, by the holder of a particular
key. Those claims are the ones your code (or the form) passed in: which service,
which operation, which controls ran and what they returned, and hashes that
commit to the input and output. It carries the hashes, not the input and output
— so it can be handed to someone outside your organisation without handing over
the data.

That is a narrower statement than "it proves what the system did", and the
difference matters. A record is not a compliance certificate, it is not an
audit result, it does not guarantee a control was effective, and it does not say
the model was right.
[What a green check does and does not establish](/verify/what-a-check-proves/)
is worth three minutes of your time before you show one to anybody.

## Two signing tiers, and one flag that cuts across them

The tier is what was cryptographically established about the record:

<div class="badge-legend">

| Tier | Meaning | What you can do with it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <ReceiptBadge kind="self-signed" /> | You signed your own record, and nobody else did. | Internal record-keeping. It is a diary. |
| <ReceiptBadge kind="witnessed" /> | Countersigned by a party that did not author it. | Hand to a third party as evidence. |

</div>

A self-signed record is not a weaker witnessed record. It is a different
claim: *"I say this happened."* A witnessed record says *"I say this happened,
and someone who is not me recorded that I said it, at a time I could not choose
after the fact."* Everything in these docs keeps those two apart.

SAMPLE is a separate axis — it describes the *subject matter*, not the signing:

<div class="badge-legend">

| Flag | Meaning | What you can do with it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <ReceiptBadge kind="sample" /> | Synthetic. The operation it describes never ran. The signature over it is real and will verify. | Learn the format, run a real verification. Never evidence of an event. |

</div>

So a sample record is always *also* a tier — <ReceiptBadge kind="self-signed" sample />
or <ReceiptBadge kind="witnessed" sample />. "Sample" never means "unsigned",
and a passing signature check on one is not a claim that anything happened.

<Aside type="note" title="What is live today">
  These Start pages describe the browser flow at
  [app.glacis.io](https://app.glacis.io). The [Connect](/connect/) path — the
  Python SDK, offline signing, provider wrappers — is published on PyPI as
  `glacis` and works offline today regardless of what the portal is doing.
  Online / witnessed mode is not generally available yet.
</Aside>
