Sample receipts show you the format. This produces one that is actually yours,
still without leaving the browser.

<Steps>

1. **Open *Mint a receipt* in your workspace.**

2. **Describe the event, or paste a payload.**

   Either a short description of an AI operation your system performs, or a
   JSON payload with the input and output you want committed to. Both end up in
   the same place: hashed locally, with only the hash leaving the page.

   <Screenshot shape="phone" caption="Mint a receipt — describe an event or paste a payload." />

3. **Mint.**

   The workspace calls the witness on your behalf and returns the receipt bound
   to your workspace.

4. **Check it.**

   Use the verify action on the receipt, or copy its verification permalink and
   open it somewhere else entirely — a different browser, a colleague's phone.
   Verification is client-side; the receipt is checked wherever it is opened.

</Steps>

## What you get back

<ReceiptBadge kind="witnessed" gloss />

A witnessed receipt is countersigned by a party that did not author it. That is
the property that makes it worth showing to someone who has no reason to trust
you: you could not have chosen its timestamp after the fact, and you cannot
quietly revise it later.

<Aside type="caution" title="When witnessing is not live, the button says so">
  Witnessing depends on the hosted witness being up. When it is not — during a
  deploy, or before the service goes live in your region — the mint action
  returns an honest *"witnessing is not available yet"* state, or a receipt
  explicitly badged <ReceiptBadge kind="self-signed" />.

  It never shows you a green <ReceiptBadge kind="witnessed" /> check that
  nothing backs. If you see a witnessed badge, a countersignature exists and you
  can check it.
</Aside>

## What this proves, exactly

It is cryptographic evidence that at a particular time your workspace generated
a specific claim — a commitment to a particular input/output pair — and that the
hosted witness recorded that claim and returned a proof of where it sits in the
log.

It does not prove the operation described actually ran in production, that the
output was correct, or that anything is compliant. You typed this one into a
form. That is fine for learning what the artefact is — it is not evidence about
your system. And how much the countersignature is worth to a sceptical reader
depends on how independent the witness is of you; that is a question about the
deployment, not about the format.

Receipts become evidence about your system when your system emits them, which
is the next page.
