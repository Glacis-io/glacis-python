title: Documentation is not evidence

Most "AI governance" is documentation: a policy PDF, a control spreadsheet, a
screenshot, a vendor questionnaire. All of it describes what a system is
*supposed* to do. **None of it proves what the system actually did** on a given
request.

## The difference

| Documentation | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| "We filter PII." | "On this covered action, the PII control ran and reported X — here is the signed record." |
| A policy document | A cryptographic record produced as a byproduct of enforcement |
| Trust the operator's word | Checkable by someone who does not trust the operator |
| Can be written after the fact | Bound to signed bytes; tampering is detectable |

## Why it matters

- Audits want proof a control ran on transactions in scope.
- Incidents need integrity you can defend.
- Regulators and insurers increasingly want claims that are checkable, not only narrated.
- Procurement stalls when buyers cannot verify vendor claims independently.

A screenshot can be staged. A policy can be aspirational. A signed record over
the actual decision cannot be quietly rewritten without detection — **within the
limits of what was signed and who controlled the keys.** See
[what a check proves](/verify/what-a-check-proves/).

<Aside type="tip">
  Test for any AI governance claim: *could a skeptical third party check it
  without taking your word for it?* If not, it is documentation, not evidence.
</Aside>
