**Attestation** here means: a signed statement about a covered AI action —
what was claimed, what control decision was reported, and enough structure for
someone else to check the signature and hash bindings — without receiving the
underlying content.

On glacis.io this artifact is called a **record**. OVERT's specification text
still says *receipt*.

## What it is not

| Nearby idea | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| Audit logs | Usually mutable operator-controlled text; not designed for independent crypto verification |
| TEE / hardware attestation | Attests a hardware/enclave measurement — complementary, not the same artifact |
| Model signing / SBOMs | Attest build/supply-chain identity of a model artifact, not a runtime decision |
| "The model is safe" | Attestation records claims about an event; it does not prove safety or correctness |

## What a useful record carries (conceptually)

- Identity of the deployment / workflow (as declared)
- Content-safe commitments (hashes) instead of raw prompts/outputs
- Reported control outcomes for covered decisions
- Signatures (and, at higher assurance, independent witnessing)

Exact fields and verification procedures are defined by [OVERT](https://overt.is)
and by the concrete profile your deployment uses.

<Aside type="caution">
  Self-signed offline SDK artifacts (`oatt_…`) are useful for learning and
  internal integrity checks. They are not automatically conforming OVERT
  documents. See [OVERT](/overt/).
</Aside>
